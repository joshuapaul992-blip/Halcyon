Display Properties

Display Properties sets the screen resolution, arranges your monitors and chooses the screen saver. Open it from the Control Panel (Display...), by right-clicking the desktop and choosing Properties, or by typing DISPLAY in the Command Prompt.

Screen resolution

The resolution is how many dots (pixels) the screen shows: more dots fit more on the screen, but make everything smaller. When Halcyon starts for the first time it asks the monitor which resolution suits it best and uses that. To pick another one, click the monitor in the picture on the Settings tab (or choose it in the Display list), choose a resolution in the Screen resolution list and click Apply. The list shows what that monitor reports it can show and your display adapter can drive; the monitor's own resolution is marked (recommended).

  • With a display adapter Halcyon has a driver for (such as virtual machines' adapters), the screen changes at once and Halcyon asks "Do you want to keep these settings?". If you do not answer within 15 seconds (perhaps because the screen went black), it goes back to the old settings by itself.
  • Other graphics cards are run through the computer's start-up firmware: Halcyon saves your choice and uses it the next time it starts, and offers to restart now.
Screen stays black after a restart? Restart, press F8 when the "Advanced Startup Options" prompt appears, and choose Safe video mode (800 x 600). Then pick a resolution your monitor can show. See Solving startup problems.

Several monitors

When more than one monitor is connected, the Settings tab shows them as numbered pictures, arranged as Halcyon uses them: the desktop stretches across all of them, and the mouse pointer moves from one to the next where their edges meet.

  • Arrange them by dragging the pictures so they match where the monitors stand on your desk. A monitor always snaps to an edge of another one. Click Identify to see each monitor's number on the monitor itself.
  • The primary monitor has the taskbar, the Start menu and the desktop icons, and new windows open on it. To change it, click another monitor and check Use this device as the primary monitor.
  • Extend my desktop onto this monitor switches a monitor on or off for Halcyon. A monitor that is connected but not used is drawn grey next to the others; dragging it next to them extends the desktop onto it too.
  • Each monitor has its own resolution: choose a monitor first, then its resolution.

Click Apply to use the new arrangement (Halcyon asks whether to keep it, as for a new resolution). It is remembered for the next start. Windows can be dragged by their title bar to any monitor; maximizing a window fills the monitor it is on. When a monitor is unplugged (or a virtual machine's window for it is closed), the windows on it move to the primary monitor; when one is plugged in, the desktop extends onto it.

Screen saver

The Screen Saver tab chooses a moving picture that appears when you have not used the computer for a while: pick one in the drop-down list (the little monitor shows it running), set Wait, and click OK or Apply. Settings opens the screen saver's own options and Preview shows it full screen. The screen saver covers every monitor. See Screen savers. Click the tab names at the top (or press Ctrl+Tab) to switch between the two tabs.

If the picture is wrong

Halcyon's display drivers (the QEMU and VirtualBox adapters, virtio GPUs, and NVIDIA graphics cards from the GeForce 8 to the GeForce 10 series) are used whenever such an adapter is found. If one of them cannot start, Halcyon keeps showing the picture the computer's firmware set up and writes the reason to \HALCYON\BOOT.LOG. To start without the display drivers, press F8 at start-up and choose Safe video mode (or Safe Mode); to turn one driver off for good, add a line such as display.disable=virtio-gpu (or bochs, nv) to \HALCYON\SETTINGS.INI. System Information lists the display adapters, their connectors and the monitors on them.

NVIDIA graphics cards. The first time Halcyon's own NVIDIA driver shows a monitor in a new resolution, it asks "Can you see this message?". Click Yes; if nobody answers within 15 seconds (perhaps because the screen went dark), Halcyon goes back to the picture the firmware set up. If the computer stops or is switched off while the driver starts, Halcyon notices at the next start and leaves the driver off, with a message in the corner of the screen; on the Settings tab, Turn on driver... tries it again at the next start. To keep using the firmware's picture for good, add nv.modeset=0 to \HALCYON\SETTINGS.INI (Halcyon still lists the card in System Information); nv.disable=1 leaves the card alone altogether.

On these cards the driver also lets the card draw the mouse pointer, shows each new picture only between two screen refreshes (no tearing when you drag a window), and drives up to four monitors at once (two on most older cards), one per connector. If the pointer, the picture or a second monitor misbehaves, one of these lines in \HALCYON\SETTINGS.INI narrows it down: nv.hwcursor=0 (Halcyon draws the pointer itself), nv.flip=0 (no waiting for the refresh), nv.heads=1 (one monitor only).

On GeForce 600 to GeForce 10 cards the driver also hands the copying of each new picture to the screen to the card itself, about ten seconds after the desktop appears; nothing should look different, and System Information says Acceleration: in use. If the picture shows stale or wrong patches, nv.accel=0 in \HALCYON\SETTINGS.INI turns only this off. If the computer stops while it starts, the next start leaves it off (and the driver on); deleting the line display.nv.accel.guard=blocked from \HALCYON\SETTINGS.INI tries it again.
