Working with windows

Every program shows its work in a window. All windows work the same way, so once you know one, you know them all.

The parts of a window

  • The title bar at the top shows the window's name. Drag it to move the window.
  • The three buttons at the right of the title bar minimize the window (it waits on the taskbar), maximize it to fill the screen (click again to restore it) and close it. Double-clicking the title bar also maximizes and restores.
  • Drag any edge or corner to resize a window.
  • Scroll bars appear when there is more to see than fits; the mouse wheel scrolls too.

Switching between windows

Click any part of a window to bring it to the front, or click its button on the taskbar. From the keyboard, press Alt+Tab to cycle through the open windows. Alt+F4 closes the window in front (with nothing open, it asks whether to turn the computer off).

Menus

Most programs have a menu bar under the title bar. Click a menu name to open it, or press Alt (or F10) and use the arrow keys. The underlined letter in a menu name opens it together with Alt: for example Alt+F opens the File menu. Items that cannot be used right now are shown in grey, and a key combination next to an item, such as Ctrl+S, does the same thing without opening the menu.

Right-click almost anything to get a short menu of what you can do with it.

Dialog boxes

When a program needs to ask you something, it shows a dialog box. Fill it in and press OK (or Enter), or Cancel (or Esc) to leave everything as it was. Use Tab to move between the parts of a dialog. While a dialog is open, its program waits for your answer.
