Keyboard and input languages

Keyboards differ from country to country: a German keyboard has Ü and ß, a French one starts with AZERTY. Halcyon knows 20 layouts, the same ones Windows uses: US, US-International, UK, German, Swiss German, French, Belgian, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Brazilian, Swedish/Finnish, Norwegian, Danish, Dutch, Polish (Programmers), Czech, Turkish Q, Russian and Greek.

Adding a layout

  1. Open the Control Panel and click Keyboard... (the window is called Text Services and Input Languages).
  2. Click Add..., choose a layout and click OK.
  3. To start with a different layout every time, select it and click Set as Default.
  4. Try it in the Test box, then click OK.

Remove takes a layout off the list again.

Switching between layouts

When you have more than one layout, press Left Alt+Shift to switch to the next one. The indicator next to the clock (EN, DE...) shows the layout in use; click it to choose one from a menu. In the Command Prompt, KEYBOARD /L lists the layouts and KEYBOARD de switches to one.

Typing accents and special characters

  • AltGr (the right Alt key, or Ctrl+Alt) gives the third character on a key, such as € or @ on many European keyboards.
  • Dead keys such as ´ or ^ do nothing by themselves: press them, then a letter, to get an accented letter (é, ê). Press the dead key and then Space for the accent alone.
  • Keyboard shortcuts such as Ctrl+C follow the letter printed on the key, whatever the layout.

Chinese, Japanese and Korean input is not available yet.
