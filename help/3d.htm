Shards and 3D graphics

Halcyon draws 3D pictures with its own 3D library. It needs no graphics card: the processor draws every pixel, so 3D works on any computer Halcyon runs on. Two programs show it off: the game Shards and the screen saver Lantern Valley.

Playing Shards

Open Shards from the Start menu or the desktop. You steer a hover sled over a stone platform floating in the evening sky. Glowing crystal shards hover over the platform: pick them all up before the time runs out by gliding through them. Stone pillars stand in the way; bumping into one or into the glowing rim slows you down.

Every level has more shards and pillars than the one before, and a little less time for each shard. A shard is worth 100 points times the level; the seconds left when you clear a level add 10 points each. Your best score is kept.

The frame rate

Press F in Shards to see how many pictures (frames) a second the computer draws, in the bottom right corner. On a computer of the last ten years it is well above 60 at the normal window size; in a virtual machine without hardware support it can be only a few. A larger window means more pixels to draw, so the frame rate falls when you maximize it.

The Lantern Valley screen saver

Lantern Valley glides up a winding valley at dusk while paper lanterns rise from the river and drift into the sky. Choose it like any other screen saver in Display Properties. The valley and the lanterns are made up as it goes, so the flight never ends and never looks quite the same twice.

For the curious: the 3D library

The 3D library is a large part of OpenGL 1.3, the fixed-function 3D interface of games from the early 2000s: transformations, lighting with up to eight lights, textures with mipmaps and smooth filtering, two textures at once, fog, blending, the depth buffer, clipping, vertex arrays and display lists. Programs include GL/gl.h, GL/glu.h and GL/hgl.h (the link to a Halcyon window) and draw straight into their window. The pictures are drawn in squares of 64 by 64 pixels, four pixels at a time, with the processor's SSE2 instructions.
